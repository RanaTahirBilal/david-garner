A career built on what can actually be delivered

David P. Garner served as a colonel in the United States Marine Corps, including two combat tours in Vietnam, and then spent the rest of his working life on the analysis behind defence logistics decisions.

From 1989 to 2013, close to twenty five years, he worked at the Logistics Management Institute. From 2013 until 2022 he worked as an independent consultant on multinational logistics integration and defence modernisation, much of it inside NATO frameworks and alongside allied governments.

He credits what he achieved to perseverance, and to the habit of collaborating across cultures and institutions rather than around them.

Across borders

Work that only happens in the room

In 2013 he was among the first Americans to receive the Medal of Merit from the Swedish Defence Materiel Administration, an honour awarded by an allied government rather than by his own.

Teaching

Passing the subject on

Two of his Marine Corps postings were teaching posts. He taught at the Army Field Artillery School, and he served as Marine Officer Instructor at the Naval ROTC unit at the University of Notre Dame, the university where he had taken his own first degree.

Logistics is a subject learned mostly from people who have had to do it. He spent part of his service being one of those people.

Teaching in a training classroom.
